# Admissions-oriented critical assessment

This is a reviewer-style self-assessment by the implementation assistant, not independent human peer review or an admissions prediction.

## Value to an HCI / iSchool application

The strongest feature is the coherent research trajectory: a bounded discourse analysis leads to a distinct, measurable interaction question rather than an unsupported intervention claim. The artifact shows the ability to operationalize abstract concerns—trust, distrust, source transparency, uncertainty, and contextual differences—into data structures and interactions. Its separation of evidence judgments, documentation completeness, trust/distrust reflections, and intended use demonstrates measurement discipline. A working public demo, inspectable code, critical tests, and a prospective protocol make the proposal easier to assess than a concept slide.

## Material weaknesses

The claim–evidence ledger itself has not been established as novel relative to existing verification/provenance tools. Two fictional use contexts demonstrate a hypothesis but do not establish a theory of contextual differences, community behavior, or actual reliance. There is no formative user evidence, controlled evaluation, source-grounded task benchmark, or observed behavior dataset. The future comparator and consented instrumentation are not implemented. The individual workflow provides a stronger HAI/HCI fit than a direct CSCW contribution; collaboration, shared evidence negotiation, and social accountability are not studied. AI assistance means code volume is weak evidence of the applicant’s unaided engineering ability. The applicant should demonstrate ownership through explanation, critique, and a small independently understood extension.

## CV decision

**Worth including as a research prototype/software project**, linked separately from publications. Do not list it as an evaluated system, published empirical work, proven calibration intervention, or evidence that Paper 1’s communities behave differently. It complements the paper but does not replace an empirical contribution. Its application value depends on the owner being able to explain what is implemented, why the constructs differ, why the control condition matters, and what a null or adverse result would mean.

## Questions to prepare for a supervisor conversation

1. Why can a discourse label not stand in for an individual trust attitude or verification action?
2. What does a completed record tell us—and what does it fail to tell us?
3. Why might “Supported” still lead to qualified or rejected use in a particular context?
4. How would you measure actual reliance rather than an intended-use dropdown?
5. Why do both arms need the same final-response form and source pack?
6. How could a structured interface increase effort or shallow form filling without improving judgments?
7. What work is needed to establish novelty and a CSCW research contribution?
8. Which code implements the completeness rule, persistence, safe import, and context reset?

## Highest-value follow-up

First review and understand the implementation guide. Then seek institutional supervision/ethics support, conduct formative work, strengthen the related-work comparison, and pilot independently scored tasks. Do not add live model integration or dashboards merely to enlarge the feature list.
